Title: Stan Wong: Strong corporate earnings, but less room for error

# Stan Wong: Strong corporate earnings, but less room for error.

Corporate earnings remain a powerful support for equities, but the bar for companies is getting higher. Resurging oil prices and elevated bond yields have brought inflation, valuations, and central bank policy back into sharper focus, while strong earnings expectations leave less room for disappointment.

The outlook remains constructive, but the margin for error may be narrowing. With several forces pulling in different directions, selectivity is becoming increasingly important.

## Corporate profits remain the anchor .

Corporate America continues to deliver strong earnings growth. Artificial intelligence, digital infrastructure, electrification, and a broader capital-spending cycle remain important drivers, while current forecasts suggest the expansion still has runway through the remainder of 2026 and into 2027. Many of the structural investment themes driving that growth could extend well beyond that period.

Expectations have remained elevated despite higher interest rates and geopolitical uncertainty. Continued profit growth can support markets while allowing leadership to broaden beyond a relatively small group of mega-cap technology companies.

Strong results have also improved the valuation picture. Forward price-to-earnings ratios have declined from earlier levels as corporate profits have grown faster than stock prices. In other words, some of the market’s recent gains have been supported by stronger fundamentals rather than simply higher valuation multiples.

Continued earnings delivery, however, will remain important. As expectations rise, companies need to produce the growth already reflected in their share prices. This makes valuation discipline especially important, even for businesses with strong fundamentals and growth prospects.

## Oil, inflation and the Fed .

Higher crude prices raise transportation and production costs, squeeze household disposable income and can pressure corporate margins. They can also complicate the inflation outlook.

If higher energy prices keep inflation stubbornly above target, central banks may have less flexibility to ease policy and could be forced to keep rates higher for longer. In the United States, the possibility of another Federal Reserve rate hike has moved back into focus following stronger-than-expected August employment data , shifting attention to upcoming inflation readings ahead of the Fed’s mid-September meeting. 

For investors, the significance goes beyond one Fed decision. Keeping interest rates higher for longer could keep bond yields elevated and put greater pressure on equity valuations.

## Bond yields change the equation .

When high-quality bonds offer more attractive yields, equities face greater competition for capital, particularly for companies trading at premium valuations.

Higher yields do not automatically spell trouble for stocks. If they are rising alongside healthy economic growth and improving corporate fundamentals, equities can continue to perform. The more challenging environment would be persistent inflation and elevated yields occurring alongside weaker-than-expected corporate results. Current forecasts remain strong, but in that scenario, investors would be confronting both higher discount rates and softer fundamental support for equity prices.

## Less room for disappointment .

Corporate fundamentals remain healthy, but oil has resurfaced as an inflation risk, and bond yields are creating more competition for equities. There are still important offsets. U.S. money market fund assets reached a record US$7.98 trillion, according to data from the Investment Company Institute , leaving considerable dry powder that could be redeployed into risk assets if investor confidence improves, although attractive short-term yields may keep some of that capital in cash. 

Geopolitical uncertainty persists, while markets are navigating September’s historically softer seasonal backdrop and the U.S. midterm elections are drawing closer.

No single factor should dictate an investment strategy. What matters is how earnings, valuations, interest rates, inflation, liquidity, sentiment, and technical conditions converge.

Against this backdrop, I continue to favour a disciplined and active approach, with flexibility to make tactical adjustments as conditions evolve. High-quality large-cap businesses with resilient cash flow generation, durable competitive advantages and strong growth prospects remain attractive, while valuation discipline and security selection remain important.

As always, portfolio construction should reflect both the current investment environment and an investor’s longer-term objectives, including risk tolerance, liquidity requirements, time horizon, and tax circumstances. Periods of greater uncertainty reinforce the value of maintaining a long-term perspective rather than allowing short-term headlines or volatility to determine investment decisions. These decisions should ultimately be considered within the context of each investor’s broader total wealth plan.
