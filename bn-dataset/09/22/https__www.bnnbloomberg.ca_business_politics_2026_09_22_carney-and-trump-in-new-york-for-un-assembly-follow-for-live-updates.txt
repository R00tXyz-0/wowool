# Donald Trump news: President to speak at UNGA.

Prime Minister Mark Carney is in New York today for a series of high-profile meetings with world leaders and policymakers, including those from Chile, Angola, Jordan and the United Arab Emirates.

The United Nations General Assembly, or UNGA, is underway. U.S. President Donald Trump is expected to address the room within the next hour.

## Multilateralism is the way: Guterres .

Opening the assembly: outgoing UN Secretary-General António Guterres.

He spoke first about the value of a multilateral world order as a counterweight to a planet split into two major centres of power.

He called on the superpower governments of the world to recognize that “their power isn’t so super.”.

## Trump meeting not on Carney’s schedule .

Trump does not appear on Carney’s public meeting schedule.

Carney is expected to meet the presidents of Chile and Angola around noon EDT, according to the public docket . 

Following afternoon meetings with the UAE’s technology minister and the king of Jordan, he’s expected to hold a media availability just before 4 p.m. EDT.

Later on, he’ll speak at an event centred on a two-state solution in Israel and Palestine, a concept that Canada supports.

## Carney’s push for UN reform .

Carney’s attempt to bring Canada closer to Europe and strengthen ties with countries around the world will continue this week in U.S. President Donald Trump’s hometown, as he attends the 81st UNGA session in New York City.

Senior government officials who briefed reporters ahead of the trip said Carney will meet with world leaders and business leaders, but it’s unclear if the prime minister and American president will meet on the sidelines of UNGA.

As some world leaders, like Trump, move away from multilateralism, one of the things Carney will focus on is reform of the United Nations and selection of the organization’s next secretary-general. Antonio Guterres is in his second and final five-year term as head of the UN, which comes to a close at the end of this year.

“At a moment when the international order is being remade, Canada intends to help shape it,” Carney said in a statement ahead of the trip. “At the General Assembly, we will sit down with partners old and new, defend the rules that protect our sovereignty, and turn co-operation into real opportunity for Canadian workers and businesses.”.

## Sask. premier reacts to Trump potash deal .

Trump says he is working on a “massive deal” to purchase potash from Belarus.

“The pricing would be for substantially less than we are currently paying to Canada, very good news for our Farmers and Ranchers,” he posted to Truth Social on Monday . 

Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe, whose province provides the U.S. with more than 86 per cent of its potash supply from Canada, said Trump’s tentative deal is “wrong and it doesn’t make sense.”.

“More than 40 countries - Canada and its allies - have sanctions against Belarus for supporting war criminal Putin’s invasion of Ukraine. Buying blood potash from Belarus is supporting Russian aggression,” he posted to X Monday afternoon.

“Does anyone believe Belarusian blood potash shipped through Russia is going to be more affordable, more sustainable or more ethical than potash shipped from Saskatchewan - right here in North America?”.

## New labour laws coming .

On their first day back in the House of Commons after a three-month summer hiatus, the Liberals on Monday tabled a significant piece of legislation aimed at speeding up major project approvals, though it’s already raising concerns among labour groups.

Bill C-39 — dubbed the Building Canada Strong Act by the Liberals — is meant to accelerate review and approval times for major projects to get them built quicker.

The announcement Monday was made by Government House leader Steven MacKinnon, Jobs Minister Patty Hajdu, and Dominic LeBlanc, the minister for intergovernmental affairs and One Canadian Economy, and minister of internal trade.

Labour groups say they’re concerned changes to the Canada Labour Code included in the bill will have adverse consequences for workers and strip them of their right to strike.

Specifically, according to a senior government official speaking to reporters during a technical briefing ahead of the official announcement, the changes will largely affect the collective bargaining process for industries that have previously seen strike action or imposed mediation.
